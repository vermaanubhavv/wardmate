WardMate · Clinician Data Agreement

The agreement between WardMate and you, the doctor

WardMate is adopted unit by unit, resident by resident — not procured by a hospital's IT department first. So this agreement is written for the relationship that actually exists today: between WardMate and the individual clinician who creates an account, not a hospital that may not yet know the app is in use.

Version: draft-1 · not yet published Governing law: India · DPDP Act, 2023 Companion document: Sub-Processor Register
1

Who this is between

This agreement is between [legal entity name], operating WardMate (“WardMate,” “we”), and you, the doctor or resident creating an account (“you,” “the clinician”). It is not, on its own, an agreement with any hospital — if your hospital later adopts WardMate formally, a separate institutional agreement should govern that relationship and would take precedence over this one for anything it covers.

2

What each of us is, under the DPDP Act

For the patient data you enter into WardMate, you are the Data Fiduciary — the one with the actual clinical relationship to the patient, and the one responsible for having a lawful basis to hold their data (your treating role, and your hospital's own admission and consent processes). WardMate is your Data Processor: we process that data only to provide the features you use, only on the instructions this agreement and the app's own design represent, and never for a purpose of our own.

If your hospital has its own DPA with WardMate covering the same patients, that governs instead, and you are not separately liable for anything it assigns to the hospital.

3

What we actually do with it

The short version: a photo or a spoken note you capture is sent to the AI services listed in our Sub-Processor Register to be turned into structured fields, which you review before anything is saved. Nothing is auto-confirmed. The full, current list of every service that touches patient data, and what each one is sent, is kept in that companion document rather than repeated here — so it stays accurate as providers change without this agreement needing to be re-signed.

We willWe won't
Process patient data only for clinical documentation, care coordination, and the record-keeping your role requires. Sell patient data, or use it to train a model we own.
Use only the sub-processors listed in the register, and update it when that changes. Add a new class of sub-processor without updating that register.
Delete a patient's record and its evidence on the schedule described in §6, once you remove them. Keep a deleted patient's data "just in case."
Tell you within [X hours] of confirming any breach affecting your ward's data. Sit on a known breach.
4

What we're asking of you

  • Enter only patients under your own care, on the unit you're a member of.
  • Keep your sign-in and your unit's join code to yourself — anyone holding the code sees the same patient list you do.
  • Tell us, and your hospital, promptly if you believe an account or a unit's code has been compromised.
  • Continue to follow your hospital's own policies on patient records and consent — this agreement sits alongside those, not in place of them.
5

Your rights, and a patient's

You can see, correct, or remove any patient record on your unit at any time through the app itself. If a patient (or someone acting for them) asks you for a copy of what's held about them, or to have it erased, you can act on that directly — the app is how you do it, and WardMate does not stand between you and that request.

6

How long data is kept

An active patient stays on the record for as long as you keep them there. Removing a patient moves them to Trash for 7 days — recoverable the whole time — after which the record, and any photo or audio evidence tied to it, is permanently deleted, including from storage, not merely hidden from view.

7

No warranty on the clinical content

WardMate helps you document faster; it does not practice medicine and does not replace your judgment. Every AI-suggested field is exactly that — a suggestion, shown beside the transcript or photo it came from, for you to accept, edit, or discard. You remain solely responsible for the accuracy of your patients' clinical record.

8

Ending this

You may leave your unit or delete your account at any time from Settings. Your patients' records stay with the unit — they belong to the clinical team, not to any one doctor who rotates off it.

9

Changes to this agreement

A change that affects what we're allowed to do with your patients' data will be shown to you again before it takes effect, with a new version number. Continuing to use WardMate after a change that doesn't affect data handling — a formatting fix, a clarified sentence — doesn't require re-acceptance.

Contact: [insert support email] — for a question about this agreement, a data request, or to report a suspected breach.

Where this gets accepted

Onboarding already asks every doctor to self-attest their registration number and hospital before joining a unit (app/onboarding/professional-form.tsx). The natural place for this is a second checkbox on that same screen, stamping profiles.terms_accepted_at and the version they saw — one more field on a form residents already fill in, not a new step.
